# CHAPTER 6 CONCLUSION
This thesis presented an integrated, autonomous AI waiter system designed for modern restaurant automation, bridging the gap between spoken natural language interaction and precise physical robot navigation. By unifying conversational AI in Vietnamese, deterministic backend state management, multi-role web synchronization, and ROS 2-based autonomous robot navigation, the proposed system demonstrates a robust, fully local, edge-deployed solution for smart dining services.

## 6.1 Contributions
The primary contributions of this thesis are synthesized across five key engineering and architectural domains:

**Deterministic Safety Layer Around Probabilistic Generation.** To eliminate the risk of language model hallucinations in commercial operations, we introduced a rule-based validator between intent routing and backend state execution. The validator inspects every tool argument against the authoritative menu, strips off-menu items, resolves ambiguous dish references, and enforces strict ordering workflow constraints. Across 41 evaluation scenarios, the validator successfully blocked 32 invalid or hallucinated items from reaching the order cart, achieving zero off-menu item leakage without rejecting any valid request.

**Low-Latency Intent Routing and Hybrid Knowledge Retrieval.** We designed a lightweight intent classification architecture using a Multi-Layer Perceptron over frozen Vietnamese sentence embeddings. Operating at 92.9% classification accuracy on 225 held-out utterances with a median latency of 9.0 ms, the router achieves a 24-fold latency advantage over prompting the deployment language model at equivalent accuracy (217.1 ms). For menu knowledge queries, we developed a retrieval pipeline combining lexical (BM25) and dense (FAISS) search over a 256-document index. On this menu corpus, the lexical lane alone performs best (R@5 = 0.597); the dense lane is retained for non-menu FAQ queries and cross-domain generalizability. Query rewriting translates sensory Vietnamese descriptions into concrete culinary terms, raising R@5 from 0.25 to 0.75 on queries that share no vocabulary with the menu. A deterministic gatekeeper rejects queries the menu cannot answer cleanly.

**Graph-Based Agent Architecture and Real-Time Backend.** The conversational agent is structured as a multi-stage graph with domain-specialized worker nodes, equipped with a delegate escape hatch to abstain when uncertain and a circuit breaker to guarantee spoken responses. The backend orchestrator synchronizes all state updates across three web interfaces: Customer Tablet, Kitchen Display Board, and Management Panel: alongside an automated fleet dispatcher, maintaining low-latency execution across all real-time push events.

**ROS 2 Autonomous Navigation and Precision Docking.** The mobile robot platform integrates sensor fusion combining wheel odometry and inertial measurements, graph-based mapping, and autonomous navigation controllers. To resolve positioning errors on low-traction restaurant tile floors, a visual servoing docking pipeline uses wall-mounted visual markers, reducing terminal docking error from 47.77 cm down to 1.57 cm laterally and 0.30 degrees in heading at a negligible cost of 1.1 seconds per run.

**Edge Resource Optimization and Unified Memory Budgeting.** To ensure reliable cloud-independent operation on a physical mobile robot, we profiled and constrained all onboard workloads within the 8 GB unified memory pool of an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano edge computer. The ROS 2 navigation and localization stack (comprising RTAB-Map graph SLAM, Nav2 planners and costmaps, sensor drivers, and EKF odometry fusion) consumes approximately 3.7 GB, while the edge voice processing pipeline occupies a further 3.7 GB. By carefully partitioning workloads between edge perception and local backend orchestration, the system prevents memory starvation and out-of-memory process termination while maintaining concurrent real-time control loops.

## 6.2 Summary of Empirical Validation and Key Takeaways
The empirical evaluation conducted across software and hardware modules validates the overall system design against the core objectives. Overall, the quantitative findings confirm that combining specialized low-latency intent routing, hybrid retrieval, and a rule-based validation layer successfully delivers a responsive, reliable autonomous waiter experience.

Most importantly, the experimental results demonstrate the validity of the central architectural premise: deterministic boundary layers effectively insulate critical backend operations from probabilistic AI failures. Throughout end-to-end multi-turn ordering evaluations, the rule-based validator ensured zero incorrect items or corrupted cart states reached the backend database. While language model output generation occasionally omitted explicit spoken feedback in complex multi-intent turns, the underlying transaction state and kitchen ordering pipeline remained entirely secure and error-free.

## 6.3 Limitations
While the system meets its primary engineering targets, several limitations were identified during empirical evaluation.

First, while visual ArUco docking achieved 1.57 cm lateral accuracy and 0.30 degrees heading error in the dedicated service lane, this precision remains insufficient for automated physical self-charging requiring sub-centimeter mechanical pin engagement. Furthermore, docking relies entirely on optical tracking without physical contact sensors or mechanical alignment pins to verify rigid mechanical engagement.

Second, the robot operates within a dedicated, bounded service corridor with physical side barriers rather than an open dining environment. Within such narrow, single-lane tracks, fleet management and multi-vehicle traffic control could not be empirically validated on physical hardware, as the physical prototype evaluation was conducted using a single mobile robot. Consequently, multi-robot passing maneuvers, queue management, and deadlock prevention in bounded corridors remain unverified on physical hardware.

Third, the hardware platform utilizes low-cost consumer MEMS IMU sensors and differential-drive encoders. The low-cost IMU suffers from inherent gyro bias drift and sensor noise over time, which, when combined with wheel micro-slippage on smooth track surfaces, causes cumulative dead-reckoning pose error. Consequently, the navigation stack relies heavily on continuous visual landmark corrections to maintain localization accuracy during extended multi-loop operation.

Fourth, the voice processing pipeline was fully integrated on edge hardware, but word error rate under real-world restaurant ambient noise was not formally benchmarked due to the lack of an annotated Vietnamese restaurant speech corpus.

Finally, while the deterministic validator strictly guards inputs into the system, text generated by the language model back to the customer does not yet pass a deterministic validation check, occasionally leading to incomplete spoken summaries in complex multi-intent turns.

## 6.4 Future Work
Based on the empirical findings and practical limitations of this thesis, future development will focus on expanding both the physical robot platform and the conversational AI architecture. For hardware navigation, we plan to integrate mechanical guide pins and tactile contact sensors to enable sub-centimeter alignment required for automated physical battery charging stations, while extending the fleet management system to support multi-robot physical deployments with dynamic traffic control and passing bay routing within bounded corridors. For software intelligence, we aim to introduce a deterministic response grounding layer between model generation and speech synthesis to verify that spoken dish details match backend context, alongside building an annotated Vietnamese restaurant speech dataset to benchmark acoustic speech recognition under real-world ambient noise and scaling the retrieval models for multi-lingual dining scenarios.
